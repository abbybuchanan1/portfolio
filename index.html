Research perspective

The translation layer is often where participation breaks down.

People rarely interact with systems as raw intention. They translate thought into forms, workflows, documents, prompts, schedules, and institutional language. That translation has a cost.

I’m interested in the conditions that make that cost visible: ambiguity, fluctuating capacity, sensory load, organizational expectations, and tools that assume a particular way of thinking.

Working model

Human intent → translation work → system requirements

Across my current work, the research question is not simply whether a system is usable. It is how much effort the system asks people to spend becoming legible to it.

About

I research the gap between how systems are designed and how people actually experience them.

My path into UX includes service work, education, bodywork, business ownership, spatial design, and formal UX training. The throughline is observation: understanding what people are trying to do, where friction appears, and what changes when a system responds to the person rather than the other way around.
