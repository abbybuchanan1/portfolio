Research perspective

The translation layer is often where participation breaks down.

People rarely interact with systems as raw intention. They translate thought into forms, workflows, documents, prompts, schedules, and institutional language. That translation has a cost.

I’m interested in the conditions that make that cost visible: ambiguity, fluctuating capacity, sensory load, organizational expectations, and tools that assume a particular way of thinking.

Working model

Human intent → translation work → system requirements

Across my current work, I ask how much effort a system asks people to spend becoming legible to it, not only whether it’s usable.

I call that effort the invisible tax. It shows up in a form, a prompt, a permit, or a workflow built for one way of thinking, and it lands hardest on the people with the least room to pay it.

About

I research the gap between how systems are designed and how people actually experience them.

My path into UX includes service work, education, bodywork, business ownership, spatial design, and a certificate in user experience design from Cornell. The throughline is observation: understanding what people are trying to do, where friction appears, and what changes when a system responds to the person rather than the other way around.
