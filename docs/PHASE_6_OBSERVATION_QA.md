# Phase 6 — Observation Section QA

**Status:** Complete  
**Date:** 2026-08-11

## Decision
Retain one subordinate **Observation practice** page. Do not promote it into the primary navigation and do not expand it into separate photography or poetry portfolios.

## Source assessment
### Relative diptych
The diptych is the stronger portfolio asset. Its value is formal rather than methodological: the paired images ask the viewer to notice correspondence across body and landscape through contour, channel, shadow, texture, scale, and ambiguity. This is defensible supporting evidence of visual attention and comparative observation. It is not presented as UX research evidence.

### “May, 2024”
The poem accumulates observations across biological cycles, family life, political events, embodiment, place, grief, and emergence. That structure supports the portfolio’s interest in sustained observation and pattern formation. The full poem is too long, intimate, and literary to serve the hiring objective efficiently, so the portfolio uses only a short excerpt.

## Strategic boundary
The page must not imply that artistic observation and qualitative research are interchangeable. The implementation therefore states explicitly that photography and poetry are not UX methodology and that the parallel to research is limited.

## Placement
- Linked from **About** only.
- Not present in primary navigation.
- Not promoted on the homepage.
- No gallery architecture.
- No full poem embedded.

## Hiring uncertainty reduced
This page modestly reduces one uncertainty: whether the portfolio’s emphasis on observation is a recently adopted UX vocabulary or part of a longer practice of sustained attention. It does **not** reduce uncertainty about research methodology, which must be answered by Scaffold and the research initiative.

## Diminishing-return decision
Do not add more photographs, poems, artist statements, image interactions, or exhibition context. Additional creative material would shift the portfolio toward a multidisciplinary personal site and dilute the research hierarchy.

## Acceptance criteria
- Relative remains the primary visual evidence.
- “May, 2024” is represented only by a short excerpt.
- The page explicitly distinguishes observation practice from UX methodology.
- The page is accessible only through a contextual About link.
- The page contains no claims that artistic practice validates research competence.
- Removing the page remains a viable future decision if hiring-manager testing shows that it causes category confusion.
