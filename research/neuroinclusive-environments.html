Ongoing research initiative

Neuroinclusive Environments Project

What can organizations learn from environments intentionally designed to improve participation?

The project compares physical, digital, and organizational interventions across sectors to identify recurring principles that reduce unnecessary cognitive, sensory, emotional, and organizational burden.

StatusActive · Version 1.0
Current phaseLandscape analysis
ApproachPragmatic qualitative research
Unit of analysisIntentional intervention
Central premise

Participation is shaped by the environment people must move through—not only by individual ability.

The project uses “environment” broadly: buildings, interfaces, workflows, policies, communication systems, technologies, staffing, and organizational norms can all alter how much effort participation requires.

Neurodivergent experience is an especially informative lens because it can make environmental demands visible that otherwise remain unexamined. The project’s intended relevance is broader than any single diagnosis or population.

Analytical distinction
Accessibility asks:

Can someone enter or access the system?

Participation asks:

Can someone meaningfully engage with an appropriate level of autonomy, dignity, safety, and agency?

Research questions

Human experience

How do environments influence participation? What reduces unnecessary cognitive, emotional, and sensory burden?

Design process

Which processes—co-design, evidence-based design, qualitative research, occupational-therapy collaboration, post-occupancy evaluation—appear in more mature examples?

Organizational strategy

Why do organizations invest, how do they define success, and what forms of value are they trying to create?

Transferable patterns

Which principles recur across airports, healthcare, workplaces, higher education, cultural institutions, libraries, sports venues, and public systems?

Comparative framework

The project compares interventions rather than ranking sectors.

The primary unit of analysis is an intentional intervention designed to improve participation: a sensory room, quiet waiting area, wayfinding change, workplace practice, accommodation process, sensory toolkit, or communication system.

Current phase

Phase 1 is descriptive before it becomes evaluative.

Current work identifies organizations and projects, verifies sources, maps relationships, documents methodologies where available, and looks for recurring collaborators and patterns across sectors.

Completion does not require documenting every example. The stopping rule is diminishing returns: when additional entries contribute little new understanding, effort shifts toward synthesis, outreach, and publication.

Evidence practice

Observation ≠ inference ≠ hypothesis

Major findings are intended to carry explicit confidence levels. Convergence across independent sources strengthens confidence; divergence is treated as information rather than noise.

Working framework

Participation Value Model

Intentional environmental intervention → change in participation → human outcomes → organizational outcomes → future investment.

This model is provisional. The project does not assume causal relationships where the evidence does not support them.

Status and limitations

The project is currently an independent research initiative in landscape-analysis phase. Original practitioner and participant interviews are planned for later phases. Current public conclusions should therefore be read as synthesis and emerging pattern identification, not final evaluation.
